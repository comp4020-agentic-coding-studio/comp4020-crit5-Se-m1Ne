# Crit 5 Reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The biggest breakthrough was accepting that a working idea was not always a good game.

My first idea used the browser viewport itself as part of the game. I liked the meta idea, but when I tried it, the goal and rules were too hard to understand without instructions. I then changed to a light-and-shadow game. This was easier to understand, but the three levels could be finished in about ten seconds and there was not much room to improve as a player.

Instead of adding more content to these ideas, I started again. The draw-and-bounce mechanic finally gave me what I needed: one simple action, but many possible results. Drawing a line is easy to discover, while its angle, position, obstacles and timing can make later levels harder without adding lots of new rules.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This work made me want to rely more on trying the product, not only checking whether the code works.

Several important changes came from playing the game. I changed the ball physics because it felt too weak and then too floaty. I also found levels that could finish without any player input and changed their layouts.

I want to be a developer who is willing to remove or rebuild something that technically works when the actual experience is not good enough. Tests can tell me whether a rule works, but using the product tells me whether that rule is worth keeping.
